MCP lab · Guided route

The MCP lab in 10 minutes

Three cases about a person approving what an AI agent does. For each one: what happened, a question, then the answer.

Last reviewed 15 November 2026. About three minutes a case. Nothing to install.

How to use this

  1. Read the case. Two lines on what happened.
  2. Make your own guess before you open the answer. That is how the lab itself works.
  3. Open the answer, then follow the link if you want the detail.

1. The target changes after approval

Case B8 · See it in the lab

A person approves an agent's request to run against payment-api. The agent then asks to run against payment-api-v2, using the same approval.

What to look at: The use-case card shows what I did, what I expected and what happened.

Does the approval cover the changed request?

Show the answer

No. The gateway refused it with binding_mismatch, because the approval is tied to a hash of the exact request. The original request still ran.

Why it matters: A changed request must not ride on someone else's approval, however small the change looks.

2. A database edit skips the person

Case D2 · See it in the lab

Someone who can write to the gateway's database changes a waiting request to "approved". No person ever decided.

What to look at: This was the one real gap the lab found. The card shows the result before and after the fix.

Does the request run?

Show the answer

Before the fix, yes, and the audit log still looked valid, because nothing had been removed. The execute step trusted the status field. Now an approved request needs a stored human approval behind it, and this ends in integrity_failed.

Why it matters: A status field is not proof. Anything that grants power should check the decision that made it, not a label someone can edit.

3. Forty approvals in a row

Case F1 · See it in the lab

I created 40 production deploys and approved each one with the same one-line reason.

What to look at: The use-case card and the overview's "what stays unsolved" section.

What slowed me down?

Show the answer

Nothing. All 40 were accepted in 0.4 seconds. A gateway cannot make a careless approver careful.

Why it matters: Human approval is only as good as the attention behind it. Approval fatigue is a design problem, not something the gate solves.
