How I work

How I use AI, and where I stop.

AI speeds up my analysis. It does not get to make my decisions or see my employer’s data. Here are the rules I actually follow.

What I use it for

  • Structuring research, comparing options side by side and finding gaps in my own reasoning.
  • Tightening writing, especially for executive audiences where every sentence has to earn its place.
  • Analyzing how new things like MCP and AI agents change trust boundaries, permissions and non-human identity.

AI is the future, but humans can’t be replaced. The two work together, with security, governance, monitoring and collaboration.

Roshan Trivedi

The rules

  • Nothing confidential goes in. No employer, client or internal operational data. That is also why every case study here is anonymized.
  • Sources get checked. If a claim matters, I trace it to something I can link to. A confident answer is not a source.
  • A human stays accountable. AI can draft and challenge. I read it, change it and own what gets published or recommended.
  • AI advises, people decide. The same principle runs through the case studies, from access reviews to break-glass alerts: the model recommends and a person confirms.

Why it matters for identity

The way I use AI is the same way I think agents should be governed: scoped access, clear ownership, evidence of what happened, and a person who can say no. If I can’t apply that to my own workflow, I have no business recommending it to anyone else.
