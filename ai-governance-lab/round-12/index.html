Who approved, what exactly, and when (round twelve)

Round twelve is about approvals, the human step that every earlier round assumed. Who approved? Was it the person who asked? Did they approve what finally ran, or something changed afterwards? Was it still true when it ran? What if the approver left, or two approvers disagreed? It uses a small approval queue I wrote for the lab. It is not a real approval product, and no AI model is involved.

The animation below follows one request from the person who raises it to the approval, and shows where each case sits.

  3. A single denial stops it Any approver

    If one approver says no and another says yes, the request does not run. Disagreement means stop and talk.

    • Votes

    AI’s part: None.

  4. Batches leave out the big ones An approver

    Twenty small requests can be approved in one click. A request over the high-risk limit cannot be in the batch and needs its own look.

    • High-risk limit

    AI’s part: None.

    Lab cases: F3

  5. The approval lapses The approval queue

    An approval is good for 24 hours. A request run on Thursday under a Monday approval is refused and asked for again.

    • Approved at
    • 24-hour lapse

    AI’s part: None.

    Lab cases: F4

  6. The approver leaves, and the queue moves HR and the approval queue

    Requests waiting for a person who has left move to their manager, so nothing waits for someone who no longer exists.

    • Departure
    • Reassignment

    AI’s part: None.

    Lab cases: F5

  7. What nobody can see Nobody, today

    Two people can approve each other's requests and meet every rule above. The lab cannot see that arrangement. A person who looks at patterns can.

    • Approver pairs

    AI’s part: None.

    Lab cases: F7

I wrote the expected result for 7 new cases (F1 to F7) and saved them before writing any code, on 25 Oct 2026 at 04:58 IST. The first run gave 7 of 7 as I guessed. That is a warning about my guesses, not good news about the controls: they were easy. Switching off one control at a time, in a copy of the code, was noticed by the matching case 6 of 6 times. F7 is a limit, so it has no control to switch off. The files are on the run page.

What this does not show

  • The approval queue is a small model I wrote. There is no real approval product, no real people and no AI model.
  • F7 is a limit, not a fix: two people who approve each other's requests meet every rule, and the lab cannot see the pattern.
  • F5 is a thin check: it only shows that waiting requests move to the manager when the approver leaves.
  • I wrote the guesses and the code, so “as expected” shows the rules do what I intended, not that I thought of every case.
  • This round ran in a Linux sandbox with Python 3.11 and on my MacBook Air (Python 3.9.6) with the same verdicts: 7 of 7 as guessed and 6 of 6 control-off checks noticed. Both sets of files are on the run page.
