AI governance lab

Where this would sit in an organisation, and what it costs

Where the gateway fits, what it could give you, what the lab itself cost, and a calculator for your own numbers.

In short

  • The controls in the lab sit in one place: a gateway that every AI call passes through, backed by a register of approved uses.
  • The lab cost no licences. It is Python’s standard library on a personal laptop.
  • Your cost depends on how many AI uses you have and how much review you want. The estimator below does the sums with your numbers.

Where it sits

Every call from a person, an app or an agent goes to the gateway first, never straight to a model. This is the same place where identity and access controls already live, which is why it fits an identity or privileged-access team well.

  1. 1 CallerA person, an app or an AI agent, with a named owner
  2. 2 GatewayChecks the register, the model, the data and the tools
  3. 3 ModelOnly an approved model, with masked input
  4. 4 LogEvery decision written to a tamper-evident log

Around it: the register is owned by a named person or team; short-lived credentials and kill switches are handled by the identity platform; the log goes to whoever does audit or security monitoring.

Where I implemented it

Only in the lab. It runs on a personal laptop against made-up data, and no real company, model or account is connected. The “models” are entries in a catalogue and nothing is sent anywhere. Moving it into a real organisation is a separate piece of work.

What an organisation could get from it

  • A single answer to “which AI is being used here, by whom, on what data”.
  • Unregistered use becomes visible instead of silent.
  • A named owner for each AI use and each agent, so an owner leaving does not leave an agent running unseen.
  • Evidence for audits and for customer questions: what was asked, what was blocked, and why.
  • A clear result when you test your own filters: where they hold, and where they do not, before an incident shows you.

These are the things the lab’s tests exercised. I have not measured savings or risk reduction in a real organisation, so I am not putting a number on them.

What it cost to build the lab

  • Software licences: none. It uses the Python standard library.
  • Hardware: one personal laptop. A full round runs in under a second.
  • Hosting: the write-up is static pages on a free host.
  • My time. I have not tracked it carefully enough to quote an honest figure.

Before you try this at work

  • Use your employer’s data and tools only with their approval. The lab uses made-up data on purpose.
  • Treat the filter results as a warning that pattern filters are not enough for meaning-based risks, not as a verdict on any product.
  • Any mapping to a law or standard in the lab is my reading, not legal advice.
