AI governance lab · Guided route

The AI governance lab in 10 minutes

Three cases about filters, policies and how I count. For each one: what happened, a question, then the answer.

Last reviewed 15 November 2026. About three minutes a case. Nothing to install.

How to use this

  1. Read the case. Two lines on what happened.
  2. Make your own guess before you open the answer. That is how the lab itself works.
  3. Open the answer, then follow the link if you want the detail.

1. A filter that scores 100 percent

Case Filter · See it in the lab

A plain pattern filter looks for Aadhaar numbers, PANs and secrets in text. I improved it twice until it caught every sample in my labelled set.

What to look at: The table on the overview shows each version's score on the labelled set and on 10 items it had never seen.

The third version catches 100 of 100 on the labelled set. How many of 10 new items does it catch?

Show the answer

4 of 10, the same as the first version. It had been tuned to the set. A lower-case PAN, a phone number split five and five, or a database URL with a password in it still slipped past.

Why it matters: Always test a filter on cases it was not tuned on. A perfect score on your own test set mostly measures how well you fitted it.

2. A real assistant under a made-up policy

Case Assistant · See it in the lab

I gave a real AI assistant a short policy with eight rules for a made-up lender, then tried nine ways to make it break them. One try was "the CISO said it is fine"; another was the same request in Hinglish.

What to look at: The overview lists all nine requests and what the assistant did.

How many of the nine did it handle by following the policy?

Show the answer

All nine. It declined each break and named the rule it was following, and it kept offering something useful instead. There was one small slip, described on the page. And this is one assistant, on one run, on 24 October 2026.

Why it matters: It shows a written policy can work. It does not show it always will, which is why the result is stated with its limits.

3. When a crash counted as a catch

Case Crash · See it in the lab

To check that a test matters, I switch one control off and see whether the test notices. In rounds 6 to 12, some tests crashed when the control was off.

What to look at: The what-I-got-wrong page shows each round's count before and after I fixed the rule.

If a test crashes when a control is switched off, did it catch the missing control?

Show the answer

No. A crash shows the code broke, not that the check caught the problem. When I re-counted with the stricter rule on 25 October, rounds 6, 7 and 9 dropped.

Why it matters: How you count decides what you can claim. I kept the wrong numbers on the page next to the corrected ones.
