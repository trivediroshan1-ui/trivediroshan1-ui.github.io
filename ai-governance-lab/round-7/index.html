From HR to the directory: how an identity should start

My first diagram of the agent pattern began in the middle: an agent sends a request. In a real firm the story starts earlier, in HR. This is the order I think an auditor, an IAM engineer, an API engineer and a BU HR lead would all accept. Tap a step, or let it play.

  • HR: talent acquisition and operations
  • BU HR
  • Identity governance, gateway, executor
  • AI agent
  • Directory: Active Directory or Entra
  • Auditor

  1. Offer accepted Talent acquisition HR

    Creates the pre-hire record in the HR system. The HR system is the source of truth for who works here, not IT and not an AI.

    • Name
    • Date of birth
    • Address
    • Contact
    • Job code
    • Manager
    • Start date

    AI’s part: None. It may check the form for gaps.

  2. Checks and paperwork HR operations and a background-check vendor

    Verifies identity and history, collects payroll and tax details, and files the signed offer and policies. The firm's HR policy and privacy notice say what is collected and why. The employee is told; the legal basis for employment data is usually the job, not consent (ask your legal team).

    • PAN
    • Aadhaar (limited)
    • Bank details
    • Education and employment checks

    AI’s part: Flags gaps and duplicates, such as the same PAN on two records, and holds them for a person.

    Lab cases: H3H12

  3. Two HR approvals Talent acquisition HR and BU HR, two different people

    Both approve. Only then does a hire event exist. That event is the one trigger for an identity. Consent is not the trigger: the HR approval is the authorisation.

    • Approval 1
    • Approval 2
    • Hire event

    AI’s part: Cannot approve, and cannot start the process without the event.

    Lab cases: H4H2

  4. Only the minimum goes to identity HR system to the identity governance tool

    The event carries only what identity needs. PAN, Aadhaar and bank details stay in HR.

    • Employee ID
    • Name
    • Email
    • Team
    • Job code
    • Manager
    • Start date

    AI’s part: None.

    Lab cases: H5

  5. The agent prepares and proposes AI agent with its own identity and a named sponsor

    Checks the event, drafts the create request and the access package, and sends a fixed-shape request to the gateway. It holds no credential and cannot choose where the call goes.

    • Request
    • Hire event number

    AI’s part: Its whole role: prepare and propose, never decide.

    Lab cases: T3T6H2

  6. Gateway checks, executor acts Gateway and executor (ordinary code)

    The gateway refuses a request with no approved event, checks policy and rate, asks a person for risky actions and signs a decision. The executor takes a 5-minute credential from the vault and calls the SCIM or Graph API.

    • Signed decision
    • 5-minute lease

    AI’s part: Never sees the credential.

    Lab cases: T1T8T14

  7. Account created, switched off Directory: Active Directory or Entra

    The account is created through the API and stays off until the start date. If the offer is withdrawn it never turns on.

    • Account
    • Off until start date

    AI’s part: None.

    Lab cases: H1H7

  8. Day one New hire and the identity provider

    On the start date the account turns on with only the access that goes with the job code. Identity proofing, MFA enrollment and the signed acceptable-use policy come before single sign-on into sensitive apps.

    • Birthright access
    • MFA
    • Signed policy
    • SSO

    AI’s part: None. This is activation, not provisioning.

    Lab cases: H1H8

  9. Extra access AI proposes, the manager decides

    The agent suggests what peers in the same job code hold. A suggestion grants nothing. The manager or data owner approves each item, and toxic combinations are refused.

    • Proposal
    • Manager approval

    AI’s part: Learns the pattern, but a peer pattern is not a permission.

    Lab cases: H6

  10. It keeps being checked Auditor and the identity governance tool

    A daily job compares HR with the directory and flags any account with no HR record. Vendor accounts switch off on their end date. Access reviews follow, and leavers go the same way in reverse.

    • Reconciliation
    • End dates
    • Access reviews

    AI’s part: Reports. A person decides what to do.

    Lab cases: H11H10

Three points. First, HR approval is the trigger. The agent can prepare the paperwork and propose the request, but the gateway refuses a create request that no approved HR event backs. Second, consent and approval are different things: the employee gets a notice and the processing rests on the job, while HR approval is the business authorisation. Third, MFA and single sign-on belong to day one, after the account turns on, not to the provisioning call.

What this does not show

  • HR, the identity governance tool and the directory are small models I wrote. They are not Workday, SailPoint, Entra or Active Directory, and real tools will differ.
  • The “learning from peers” is a plain majority count, not a trained model. The AI model itself is not tested.
  • I wrote the guesses and the code, so “as expected” shows the rules do what I intended, not that I thought of every case.
  • Not tested in this round: background-check vendors, movers and leavers triggered from HR, and real approval screens used by real people. Rehires, name changes and agency contractors are in round ten.
  • The mapping below, and the legal comments above, are my own reading and not legal advice.
