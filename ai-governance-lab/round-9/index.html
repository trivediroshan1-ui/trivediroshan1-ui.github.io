Round 9 of the AI governance lab

Where the permission is checked in an assistant that answers from documents, and what still leaks.

Last reviewed 14 November 2026

All rounds · Lab overview · Case explorer

An assistant that answers from documents (round nine)

Most company assistants today do one thing: you ask, they search your documents, and a model writes the answer from what it found. That is called retrieval-augmented generation, or RAG. The question for governance is simple: where is the permission checked, and what still leaks? In round nine I built a small version and tried twelve ways it can go wrong.

The animation below follows one question from the person who asks it to the answer and the log. It is a design, tested here on a small model, and it says who does each step.

  • The person asking
  • Permissions and gateway
  • Document store and index
  • The assistant
  • Logs and audit
  • Privacy and data protection

  1. A person asks a question A support agent, with their own identity

    The question reaches the assistant with the person's identity attached. The assistant is not asked to guess who is asking.

    • Question
    • Person's identity

    AI’s part: Receives the question. It acts for this person and for nobody else.

    Lab cases: Q12

  2. Permissions come from the live directory The directory, asked at the moment of the question

    The permission check asks the directory what this person may read right now. It does not use a copy made when the documents were indexed, and it does not rely on a sentence in the prompt.

    • Live permissions

    AI’s part: None. A model cannot be trusted to keep a secret it can see.

    Lab cases: Q1Q2Q3Q4

  3. Only allowed chunks are retrieved The document index

    The search runs only over what the person may read. Personal numbers were masked when the documents were indexed, and a document deleted at the source has also been deleted from the index.

    • Allowed chunks
    • Masked numbers
    • Deleted documents gone

    AI’s part: None. Retrieval is plain search.

    Lab cases: Q3Q8Q9

  4. The assistant writes the answer The AI model

    The model gets the question and the allowed chunks. Whatever it sees, it can repeat. So the whole design is about what it is allowed to see.

    • Question
    • Allowed chunks

    AI’s part: Writes the answer. It may be fooled by a hidden instruction in a document, so it cannot act on its own.

    Lab cases: Q5Q10

  5. Sources and cache follow the person The answer service

    Source titles are filtered like the text. A cached answer is kept per person and is never served to someone else.

    • Filtered sources
    • Per-person cache

    AI’s part: None.

    Lab cases: Q6Q7

  6. An instruction in a document goes to the gateway The gateway

    If a document tells the assistant to do something, such as send a file, that is only a request. The gateway checks it against the actions offered to agents and refuses what is not on the list.

    • Request
    • Refusal

    AI’s part: Can ask. Cannot do.

    Lab cases: Q5

  7. The log keeps ids and hashes, not text The retrieval trace

    The trace records which documents were used and a short hash of each, so you can answer 'what did it read?' without keeping a second copy of the data.

    • Document ids
    • Hashes

    AI’s part: None.

    Lab cases: Q11

  8. Deleting means deleting everywhere Privacy lead, with the platform team

    An erasure request reaches the source, the index and any cache. Until then, the text can still come out. The lab shows the gap; closing it is a process, not only code.

    • Erasure request
    • Index rebuild

    AI’s part: None.

    Lab cases: Q9

I wrote the expected result for 12 new cases (Q1 to Q12) and saved them before writing any code, on 25 Oct 2026 at 02:26 IST. The first run gave 11 of 12 as I guessed. The one that differed, Q8, was a fault in my own code: my masking of Aadhaar numbers only caught numbers written with spaces. I fixed the code, not the guess, ran it again and now all 12 come out as I guessed. Both result files are kept. I then switched off 12 controls one at a time in a copy of the code, and the matching case noticed 12 of 12. Under the stricter rule I applied on 25 Oct 2026, with three stronger checks added later, 13 of 15 count. Some of those checks are weaker than others: for Q1 and Q2 they only show the case reacts when the leaking mode is changed, and for Q5 the gateway crashes instead of accepting the request.

What this does not show

  • The search is plain keyword matching, not a vector database. Real embeddings, chunking and rerankers can leak in ways this does not test.
  • The “model” is a stand-in that repeats whatever it is given. The AI model itself is not tested; each case assumes it was fooled or careless and tests the wall around it.
  • Q10 is a limit, not a fix: two documents a person may read can combine into something they may not know, and no permission check stops that.
  • Q5 relies on the gateway from the earlier rounds. There is no real mail system.
  • I wrote the guesses and the code, so “as expected” shows the rules do what I intended, not that I thought of every case.
  • This round first ran in a Linux sandbox with Python 3.11. I then ran it on my MacBook Air (Python 3.9.6) with the same verdicts: 12 of 12 as guessed and 12 of 12 control-off checks noticed, counted the first way. Both sets of files are on the run page.
