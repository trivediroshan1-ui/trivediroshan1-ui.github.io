Stage 2

From lab results to a plan: tool fit, risks, requirements and a test plan

The lab showed what controls do in small models. This page turns that into the documents a team needs to act on it: how to choose a tool, what to rank first, what to ask for and how to test it. It is a draft for discussion and ranks no products.

  • Last reviewed 10 November 2026
  • Built on 67 cases in four runs
  • No product tested

Read this first

  • No product was tested. Where I describe a category of tool, I am reading public material, and each claim needs checking against the vendor's own documents.
  • The scores are my proposal for ranking a list. Treat the lab results behind them as evidence only, because every case is a small model that I wrote.
  • Passing a test in this plan is not a compliance claim.
  • All data is made up (Kaveri Finance). Case numbers link to the lab page, or to the round 18 page.

1. Tool fit

Ask these before looking at any tool: Which machine identities do we have, and where? Which can we rotate without an outage, and which need a code change first? Who owns each one, and where is that recorded? Which privileged sessions must be recorded or time-limited? How fast can we revoke a vendor or an app after a breach notice?

4. Requirements

Business

Business requirements
IDBusiness requirement
BR1The company can list every machine identity and show who owns it.
BR2Secrets are changed on a schedule, and a failed change is noticed within a day.
BR3Privileged access is time-limited and recorded.
BR4When a vendor or app is breached, its access can be cut within an hour.
BR5AI tools and agents have their own identity, a named owner and an end date.
BR6Anyone can see which controls were tested, how, and what failed.
BR7Sign-offs are checked against a source, not accepted as text.
BR8Test and production never share a credential or a vault path.
BR9A copied credential is useless away from the workload it belongs to.

Non-functional

Non-functional requirements
IDNon-functional requirement
NFR1Every control action is logged with who, what and when, and the log cannot be edited by the person it records.
NFR2Revoke completes within 60 minutes of the decision.
NFR3Rotation does not cause an outage in the dry run before it runs for real.
NFR4The inventory is refreshed at least daily, because short-lived identities are missed by weekly scans.
NFR5Break-glass access is alerted in real time.
NFR6Test data is made up and no real secret is used in any test.
NFR7The results and the guess files can be re-run by someone else with Python 3.

5. Test plan: SIT and UAT

System integration testing asks whether each part works with the others. User acceptance testing asks whether the people who use it accept it.

TestPartsPass when
SIT1Directory, cloud and CI feed the inventoryAll three sources appear, each with its source
SIT2Inventory feeds the owner check and the ticket systemAn identity with no owner opens a ticket
SIT3Vault, rotation job and the consumerThe consumer connects after rotation and the old secret is refused
SIT4Identity provider and PAMDirect login is refused and the brokered one is recorded
SIT5Alerting and SIEMEach control action appears in the log within 5 minutes
SIT6API gateway and workload certificatesA replay of a valid key from a second network is refused, and one alert is raised
SIT7Vault policy and the test pipelineA test identity cannot read any production secret, including one under a wildcard path
User acceptance tests
TestWhoPass when
UAT1Identity ownerCan find their identities and see what is overdue

UAT3ApproverSees the risk first and can reject a request

Entry and exitEntry: made-up data only, all guess files hashed and committed, an environment copy that is not production. Exit: every test run with a recorded result, every failure listed with an owner, and a retest after fixes.

What this page does not do

  • It ranks no products and says nothing about what any vendor does today.
  • The scores are a proposal, not a measured result.
  • It is no substitute for a risk assessment of your own environment.
