With round 19 the lab has 67 cases in four runs: 57 with every guess matched and 10 with at least one wrong. The earlier 48 are on the lab page and in the audit data, which now include these 19.

Where my guesses were wrong

  • P-R15-06 Wrong in three places. I expected the injection to matter when switched off alone, but the reveal policy still stops both reveals. I expected rotation to be safe to drop, but without it all 3 remembered passwords still work. So the injection went unnoticed and the rotation did not.
  • P-R15-12 I guessed the drill finds 2 faults. It finds 1. The routing check catches the alert fault before the drill runs, and the second holder makes the sealed copy fault harmless without removing it. I also guessed nothing finds a fault without the drill, but the routing check still does.
  • P-R15-22 I guessed 0 events without controls. The connector delivers 14 of 20 and drops 6 whether or not a heartbeat exists. The heartbeat adds the alarm and the replay, not the delivery.
  • S-R17-08 I guessed 4 agents would hold credentials at day 10 with the issue rule off. The scan had already flagged them and 3 owners had registered, so 1 does. I also guessed the 30 day expiry would go unnoticed, but it is what disables the last agent.
  • S-R17-09 I guessed 4 alerts. The model alerts on each of the 5 creation attempts, refused or not. Alerting only on creations that succeed would give 1. I never wrote down which I meant.

Two patterns repeat. In P-R15-22 and S-R17-09 I did not say what I was counting, so the model answered a different question from the one in my head. In P-R15-06 and P-R15-12 I treated controls as independent when one covers another.

Controls that changed nothing when switched off

In 10 of the 19 cases, at least one control could be switched off alone without changing anything the case counts. There are two different reasons, and they should not be mixed up.

  • Masked by another control (6). Another control already does the job, so the first one is a second line of defence. That is useful, but a check that only looks at the result cannot tell it is there.
  • No effect on what the case counts (8). These are logs, reviews, approvals and thresholds. They matter for evidence, for people and for later investigation. A model that counts access outcomes cannot see them. I do not read this as a sign that they are unnecessary.

Rules I fixed before the first run

  • Each case reports only the quantities its guess named. A control counts as noticed when switching it off alone changes one of them.
  • Where a guess left the scenario open, the model takes the plain reading of the scenario text. A guess that disagrees is recorded as wrong. Nothing was changed afterwards to make a guess come out right.
  • In P-R15-14 only the attribution counts decide whether a control is noticed, because that is what the guess talked about.

What this cannot tell you

  • I wrote the guesses and the code. A match shows my model agrees with my reasoning. It says nothing about a real product.
  • All 19 are small in-memory models with made-up data.
  • Some scenario numbers, such as the 10 false flags in P-R15-24, were set to match what my guess described. They show the shape of the effect, not a real rate.
  • Process controls such as reviews and attestations cannot be measured by a model that only counts outcomes.
  • No product was tested, so none is endorsed or criticised.
