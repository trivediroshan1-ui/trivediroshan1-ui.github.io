Step 2: Own

A named person, a purpose and an end date for each.

An identity that no person answers for cannot be kept. This step gives each identity a named owner, a purpose and an end date, and says what happens when one is missing.

What the cases in this step showed

  • A key with no owner stayed active on day 31. With a rule it was paused on day 31.
  • A front door that refuses new identities without an owner, scope and end date stopped the problem at creation.
  • Four agents on one shared key left the log unreadable. One key per agent named the agent.
  • A vendor engineer who left their company still had an active account at 120 days. A sponsor review every 30 days disabled it.

A question to ask

If a credential in your estate has no owner today, who finds out, and when?

8 cases follow. All guesses in this step matched. Controls nothing noticed: N-R14-06, N-R14-10.

N-R14-09 Identity with no owner

Step 2: Own · Run 1 · Own and retire · Guess matched

A key is found with no named owner.

Diagram for case N-R14-09: see it on the lab page.

GUESSMatched in both runs (control off, control on).

TAKE-AWAYWith no rule, an ownerless key stays active. A rule that pauses it on day 31 turns a silent risk into a ticket someone has to answer.

N-R14-18 Front door for new identities

N-R14-21 Agents share a team credential

Step 2: Own · Run 2 · Own and retire · OWASP NHI9 · Guess matched

4 agents use one shared key. One agent misbehaves.

Diagram for case N-R14-21: see it on the lab page.

GUESSMatched in both runs (control off, control on).
MEASUREDWithout: log names agent no, revoke hits 4. With: log names agent yes, revoke hits 1.
TAKE-AWAYFour agents on one team key: the log says "team key" and a revoke hits all four. One key per agent makes the log name the agent and the revoke hit one.

N-R14-06 An identity created outside the normal process

Step 2: Own · Run 4 · Own and retire · Guess matched · Control masked · Limit recorded

10 service accounts are created in a month. 7 go through the front door (owner, scope, expiry). 3 are created straight in the cloud console by developers to unblock a release.

Diagram for case N-R14-06: see it on the lab page.

GUESSMatched in both runs (control off, control on).
MEASUREDWithout: created 10, refused 0, ownerless day 1 3, first found day none, removed day none, ownerless day 30 3. With: created 7, refused 3, ownerless day 1 0, first found day none, removed day none, ownerless day 30 0.
EXTRA SCENARIOPolicy off, sweep and expiry on. Result matched my guess.
TAKE-AWAYThe cloud policy refused the 3 accounts made in the console, so the weekly sweep and the automatic expiry had nothing left to find. With the policy off they worked: the sweep found the accounts on day 7 and the expiry removed them on day 14. While the policy is on, no result shows that those two are there. An owner tag that names a real person who never agreed passes all three.

N-R14-10 The owning team is disbanded

Step 2: Own · Run 4 · Own and retire · OWASP NHI1 · Guess matched · Control masked · Limit recorded

12 service accounts are owned by a team called Payments Ops. The team is disbanded on day 0.

Diagram for case N-R14-10: see it on the lab page.

GUESSMatched in both runs (control off, control on).
MEASUREDWithout: queued day0 0, claimed by day14 0, paused day14 0, outages 0, active with old owner day90 12. With: queued day0 12, claimed by day14 9, paused day14 3, outages 1, active with old owner day90 0.
EXTRA SCENARIOOrphan queue off. Result matched my guess.
EXTRA SCENARIO14 day limit off. Result matched my guess.
TAKE-AWAYThe orphan queue listed all 12 accounts of the disbanded team, 9 were claimed within 14 days, and the 14 day limit paused the other 3, which caused 1 outage. The quarterly attestation changed none of the numbers the case counts. A mailing list nobody reads still counts as a valid owner.
