Machine identities, tested in public · Chapter 13

The market in 2026

Four deals, one new identity type and a standards project. Checked on 2 November 2026.

In the first half of 2026, four deals closed in which larger security companies bought privileged access, machine access or non-human identity specialists. Microsoft documented identity objects for AI agents. NIST asked the public how to identify and authorize agents. Each fact below has a date, a source and a label for how far I trust it.

  • 4 acquisitions closed, 11 Feb to 29 Jun 2026
  • 600+ commenters on NIST's agent identity concept paper
  • 10 risks in the OWASP NHI Top 10 (2025)
  • 0 products I tested

How to read the labels

Primary: the party's own page or document. Secondary: someone else reporting or analysing it. Vendor: a sales or press statement I could not check. Opinion: a view and not a measurement. The trust label (high, medium or low) says how much I would rely on it. Prices, ownership and roadmaps are the facts most likely to be wrong or to change. Check the vendor's own document before you quote any of them.

Where sources disagree

A data aggregator lists the Delinea and StrongDM deal on 15 January 2026 [S6]. Delinea's own release says it completed on 5 March 2026 [S2]. I used the company's release.

AI agents get their own identity objects

Microsoft's documentation for Entra Agent ID, last updated 1 May 2026, describes an agent identity as a special service principal. It has no credentials of its own. A reusable template called a blueprint creates it and acquires tokens for it. It can record a sponsor, the person or group accountable for the agent, who is contacted if there is an incident [S7]. This is vendor documentation: it describes the product and not how well it works.

Why it matters here: in the lab, four agents shared one team key, so the log said only "team key" and a revoke hit all four (N-R14-21). With one key per agent, the log named the agent and the revoke hit one. A sponsor plus an identity per agent is the product-side version of that control. I tested a model of the idea. I did not test Entra.

OWASP Non-Human Identities Top 10, 2025

The ten risks, in OWASP's order [S8]: NHI1 Improper Offboarding; NHI2 Secret Leakage; NHI3 Vulnerable Third-Party NHI; NHI4 Insecure Authentication; NHI5 Overprivileged NHI; NHI6 Insecure Cloud Deployment Configurations; NHI7 Long-Lived Secrets; NHI8 Environment Isolation; NHI9 NHI Reuse; NHI10 Human Use of NHI. NIST NCCoE: software and AI agent identity and authorization

NIST's National Cybersecurity Center of Excellence published a concept paper on 5 February 2026 and took comments until 2 April 2026 [S9]. In September 2026 NIST said it had heard from more than 600 commenters, and that its DevSecOps project will provide the first implementation use case: showing how AI agents can be identified, authenticated and authorized in the software development lifecycle [S10]. A concept paper is not guidance. No control in it is yet something you can be audited against.

The Salesloft Drift incident

Google's threat intelligence group reported that, from as early as 8 August 2025 through at least 18 August 2025, an actor it tracks as UNC6395 used stolen OAuth tokens tied to the Salesloft Drift app to read data in customers' Salesforce accounts. Google saw the actor look for AWS access keys, passwords and Snowflake tokens. Salesloft and Salesforce revoked all active access and refresh tokens on 20 August 2025. On 28 August Google added that tokens for Drift's email integration were also compromised and advised customers to treat every token connected to Drift as potentially compromised [S11]. An AppOmni post says more than 700 organizations were affected and cites an FBI advisory [S12]. I have not seen the FBI document.

Where sources disagree

One vendor blog dates the same campaign to June 2026 [S13]. Google's advisory says August 2025 [S11]. I used Google and left the other out of the facts.

Other signals, with lower weight

  • Tuhin Banerjee argues on the Cloud Security Alliance blog (23 April 2026) that inventory-first discovery is losing ground to short-lived identities, and that ownership and fast revocation are the neglected parts [S14]. Opinion.
  • Vercel confirmed unauthorized access in April 2026. Reports say it began with a third-party AI app that an employee had linked with broad permissions [S15]. Secondary, low trust.
  • GitGuardian's 2026 report counts 28,649,024 new secrets on public GitHub, 1,275,105 of them AI-service secrets, and 24,008 unique secrets in MCP configuration files [S16]. Vendor research.
  • Palo Alto Networks says machine identities outnumber human identities by more than 80 to 1, and that nearly 90% of organizations have suffered an identity-centric breach [S1]. These are the company's statistics in a press release. I did not find the underlying study.
