NHI lab · Guided route

The non-human identity lab in 10 minutes

Three cases about the accounts, keys and agents that are not people. For each one: what happened, a question, then the answer.

Last reviewed 15 November 2026. About three minutes a case. Nothing to install.

How to use this

  1. Read the case. Two lines on what happened.
  2. Make your own guess before you open the answer. That is how the lab itself works.
  3. Open the answer, then follow the link if you want the detail.

1. Where do service accounts hide?

Case N-R14-01 · See it in the lab

In the lab's made-up company, service accounts are spread across three sources. A quick inventory often looks in only one: the directory.

What to look at: The case card shows the count without the control (directory only) and with it (all three sources merged).

If you scan only the directory, how many of the 27 service accounts do you find?

Show the answer

12 of 27. Merging all three sources found all 27, each tagged with where it came from.

Why it matters: An inventory built from one source misses more than half. You cannot own, rotate or retire what you have not found.

2. Four agents share one team key

Case N-R14-21 · See it in the lab

Four AI agents call the same service with one shared team key. One of them starts doing something it should not.

What to look at: Compare what the log says, and what a revoke does, with and without one key per agent.

What does the log tell you about which agent it was, and what happens when you revoke the key?

Show the answer

The log only says "team key", and revoking it stops all four agents. With one key per agent, the log names the agent and the revoke stops only that one.

Why it matters: Shared credentials make every incident bigger: you cannot tell who did it, and you cannot stop one without stopping all.

3. A key copied to another network

Case N-R18-01 · See it in the lab

A working key is copied and used from somewhere else. The calls look exactly like the real ones.

What to look at: This is one of the cases where my guess was wrong. The audit page lists what I expected next to what happened.

With no binding in place, can the log tell the genuine calls from the copied ones?

Show the answer

No. 12 calls were accepted and the log could not tell them apart. Binding the key to its workload refused 6 and raised 1 alert. I had guessed 3 alerts, so my guess was wrong, and it stays on the page.

Why it matters: A key that works from anywhere is a key anyone can use. Tying it to the workload turns a silent copy into a refusal.
