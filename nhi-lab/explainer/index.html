Machine identities in about a minute

A short animation of the idea behind the lab: give every machine identity an owner and an end date, change secrets in the right order, and switch each control off once to see if anything notices. 62 seconds, no sound.

Animation

Every service account, API key and AI tool login is a machine identity. Most companies hold many more of them than they have people.

If your device is set to reduce motion, the animation starts paused and the scenes do not slide in. Use Next to step through. With scripts off, all six scenes show one after another.

Transcript

  1. More keys than people. Every service account, API key and AI tool login is a machine identity. Most companies hold many more of them than they have people.
  2. Many have no owner. For a lot of them, nobody knows who owns it, when it ends, or what it can reach. A leaked one can be used for months.
  3. A front door for new ones. Put a front door in front of new identities. No owner, no scope, no end date, no account. In the lab this stopped the problem when the identity was created.
  4. Change secrets in order. Update the application first, then change the secret, then check the old one is dead. In one test, five tickets closed and two old secrets still worked.
  5. Switch each control off once. Then switch each control off, one at a time, and see if anything notices. Eight cases had a control that nothing noticed. Another control was covering it, or it did not matter.
  6. What I found. I tested 46 cases on made-up data. My guesses were wrong in four places. The data and the script to check it are on the audit page.
