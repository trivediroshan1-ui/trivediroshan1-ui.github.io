Where I am now

I own an enterprise credential-management platform and its privileged access (PAM) product. In practice that means the architecture, the service design, the standards and the roadmap, and just as much the unglamorous work of getting teams to adopt it and being able to show an auditor how it works.

Most of my thinking lately goes into a simple question: what happens to identity when the “user” is a service account, a pipeline or an AI agent? That is where this site’s research and writing come from.

AI is the future, but humans can’t be replaced. The two work together, with security, governance, monitoring and collaboration.

How I got here

2011–2014

Started in enterprise technology and ERP delivery, and moved into identity work early. It taught me that access problems are usually process problems.

2014–2017

Hands-on with privileged access: onboarding accounts and service accounts into a vault, directory integration, then identity governance, single sign-on and session recording.

2017–2021

Risk governance and global delivery, including an on-site assignment in the UK. I learned how the same control reads very differently to an engineer, an auditor and a business owner.

2021–2024

Architecture and consulting leadership across India and Australia: PAM strategy, migration plans, onboarding waves, and a current-state assessment and roadmap for a large financial-sector client.

2024–2025

Principal engineering and a PAM Center of Excellence, working across more than one PAM platform.

Now

Owning the credential-management platform and the PAM product, with a growing focus on non-human identity and agentic AI.

How I work

  • Make secure access usable, governable and explainable. If people route around a control, the control has failed.
  • Treat identity as the control plane, and start from what the auditor will need to see, not from the tool.
  • Keep things public by design. The case studies here are anonymized and leave out employers, clients and internal data on purpose.
  • Use AI as a co-worker, with privacy, source checks and a human accountable for the result.

How I use AI, and where I stop

AI speeds up my research and writing. It does not make my decisions, and it never sees my employer’s or a client’s data.

  • Nothing confidential goes in. That is also why every case study here is anonymized.
  • Sources get checked. If a claim matters, I trace it to something I can link to.
  • A person stays accountable. AI can draft and challenge. I read it, change it and own what gets published.

It is the same rule I want for AI agents: scoped access, clear ownership, a record of what happened, and a person who can say no. The full page.

Right now

Moving the credential platform from standing privilege to time-bound access. On my mind outside work:

  • Who owns a service account or API key after the person who created it moves on.
  • AI agents and MCP servers as a new kind of identity that needs scoped, short-lived credentials.
  • Showing IAM controls to auditors as evidence, not as a diagram.
