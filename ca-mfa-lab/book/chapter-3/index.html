Who signs in? · Seven links every sign-in passes through

The framework: the access chain

Every sign-in, and every action an agent takes for a person, passes through seven links. A case belongs to the link where its deciding control sits. A weak link anywhere lets the bad outcome through, however strong MFA is at link 2.

  1. 1. Enroll and recover Who can add, move or reset a way to sign in? 17 cases.
  2. 2. Prove How strong is the proof at sign-in? 14 cases.
  3. 3. Trust Who vouches for the user, and is that voucher checked? 21 cases.
  4. 4. Decide Does a rule cover this sign-in, and does it say the right thing? 33 cases.
  5. 5. Issue What does the token allow, for how long, and to whom? 16 cases.
  6. 6. Act Is the sensitive action checked when it happens, not only at sign-in? 14 cases.
  7. 7. Watch and end When something changes, does access actually stop? 15 cases.

Figure 1. The access chain. Read left to right, top row then bottom row. Each box shows the question the link asks and how many cases sit in it.

Two counts stand out. Link 4, Decide, holds a quarter of the cases and half the wrong guesses. Link 6, Act, has the highest share of cases where switching off one control on its own let the bad outcome in: 12 of the 13 run.
