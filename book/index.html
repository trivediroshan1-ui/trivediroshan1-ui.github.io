Preface

I did not start as an AI person

I began with a day job and a feeling that something big was moving past me. When I first opened ChatGPT, Gemini and Claude, I did not know what to ask, how to use them, or how safe they were. So I got curious. I watched random videos, argued with the answers, tried the tools on my phone and laptop, and used what my years in identity and security had taught me to keep asking one thing: what could go wrong here?

Slowly the buzzwords became things I understood: LLMs, agents, RAG, MCP. The first agent I built made me laugh at myself, and that laugh turned into a wish to build something useful. MCP gave me the place. I wrote down what I expected, built a small approval gateway, and tried to break it, three times over. Twelve of my guesses were wrong, and they are some of the most useful pages in the book.

After 180 days I still have more to learn than I have learned. So this is a learner’s lab notebook, not a verdict. I hope it helps people learn, and helps organisations keep a person in the loop instead of trusting AI completely. If you find a case I missed, or a mistake, please tell me. I would rather fix it in the open.

My thanks to my mentor, and to every white paper, article and stranger who explained something for free.

Roshan TrivediBengaluru, October 2026

What is inside

Three parts, in plain order

Start with the one-page summary at the front. Each case in the middle says what I did, what I expected, what happened and why it matters. An index at the back lists every case by id.

What to take away

  • Findings and fixes
  • Mapping to the OWASP MCP Top 10
  • A checklist of 13 questions for your own approval step
  • Limits, decisions and closing the chapter
  • Appendices, glossary, references, index
Who it is for. Security leads, IAM and PAM teams, auditors, and anyone who has to say yes to AI agents in a company. It assumes you know what a login is. It does not assume you know MCP.
What it does not show. Everything ran on made-up tools and made-up data, once each, on my own machines. It shows what one gateway did on these cases. It does not prove anything safe.
